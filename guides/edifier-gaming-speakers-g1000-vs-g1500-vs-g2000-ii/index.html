Lowest price

Edifier G1000

Desktop gaming speakers

The G1000 is the cheapest of the three at $79.99. Its spec sheet lists Bluetooth 5.3, USB audio, and an AUX input, with two sound modes (Game and Music) and 12 RGB lighting effects. The box holds the speakers, an audio cable and a manual.

Edifier's marketing copy mentions 10W of output, but the spec sheet lists total RMS output as 2.5W + 2.5W. We use the spec sheet figure, because RMS is the number that describes continuous power.

Good: Lowest price; three ways to connect; simple Game and Music modes.
Watch for: Small output on the spec sheet; Bluetooth audio codec is SBC only; at the time we looked, only the white version was in stock.

Most features for the money

Edifier G1500

Desktop gaming speakers

The G1500 costs $10 more and adds a third sound mode (Movie), plus a listed size of 104 x 168 x 115 mm and a weight of 0.98 kg. It also includes both a USB cable and an AUX cable in the box, and lists Bluetooth, AUX and USB inputs.

Its power is the same as the G1000 on paper: 2.5W + 2.5W RMS through 2.5-inch drivers.

Good: Three sound modes; both cables included; the size is published so you can check your desk.
Watch for: Same low spec-sheet power as the G1000; signal-to-noise is listed at 80 dB(A), a little lower than the G1000's 85 dB or better.

Most powerful

Edifier G2000 II

Compact desktop speakers

The G2000 II is the step up at $129.99. Its spec sheet lists 8W + 8W RMS, which is about three times the G1000 and G1500, and a larger 2.75-inch driver with a composite bio-diaphragm. It has Game, Music and Movie modes and a listed weight of 1.4 kg.

Edifier's page also says 16W RMS in its marketing copy, while the spec sheet says 8W + 8W. Again we use the spec sheet. The page does not list the input types, so check the store page before you buy if you need a specific connection.

Good: Largest driver and highest rated power here; three sound modes.
Watch for: Bluetooth 5.1 instead of 5.3; the inputs are not listed on the product page; it costs $40 more than the G1500.

How to choose between them

Start with where the speakers will sit. Speakers this small are made for a desk about an arm's length away, not for filling a room. Their spec sheets start at 93 Hz or 98 Hz, so none of them will give you deep bass. If you want that, plan on a subwoofer or headphones for movies and loud games.

If you mostly want clearer sound than a monitor's built-in speakers and a few lights, the G1000 or G1500 is enough, and the extra $10 for the G1500 buys you a third sound mode and a published size. If you play loud or want more headroom, the G2000 II's higher rated power is the reason to pay more.

Check your connections too. All three list Bluetooth, and the G1000 and G1500 also list USB and AUX inputs. If you need to plug your PC in with a cable for the lowest delay in games, confirm the input on the store page first.

Who should skip these

  • Anyone who wants a big, room-filling sound or a subwoofer. These are desk speakers.
  • Anyone who needs a published warranty term before buying. The product pages only link to a warranty page, so read it first.
  • Anyone who only plays with a headset. Speakers add nothing for you.

Questions

Which one is the loudest?

On the spec sheets, the G2000 II: 8W + 8W RMS compared with 2.5W + 2.5W for the G1000 and G1500.

Do they work with a PC?

The G1000 and G1500 pages list USB, AUX and Bluetooth inputs. The G2000 II page lists Bluetooth 5.1 and includes a USB cable and an audio cable in the box, but does not list its input types.

Why do the prices here differ from other sites?
